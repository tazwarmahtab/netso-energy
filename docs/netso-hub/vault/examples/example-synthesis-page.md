# Pricing Disruption Creates a Switching Window in Mid-Market GTM

## Insight

The simultaneous repricing of HubSpot (per-seat increase) and Salesforce (outcome-based agents) is creating a 6-12 month window where mid-market GTM buyers are actively reevaluating their stack, and smaller vendors who price on usage or outcomes can win deals they'd normally lose to incumbents.

## Evidence Chain

1. [[pricing-model-shifts]] shows that 4 of 7 tracked vendors changed pricing models in the last 7 months. This isn't normal. The typical rate is 1-2 pricing changes per year across the competitive set.

2. [[hubspot]] moved from contact-based to per-seat pricing in September 2025. For teams with many seats but moderate contact volumes (common in mid-market), this increased costs 20-40%. Source: [[2026-04-05-customer-churn-analysis-q1]]

3. [[salesforce]] launched Agentforce at $2/conversation in January 2026, introducing outcome-based pricing to the CRM market for the first time. This forced every other vendor to explain why their pricing model is better for the buyer.

4. [[buyer-signals-mid-market]] shows that "pricing review" is now the #2 trigger for GTM tool evaluation in mid-market companies (behind "new VP of Sales hire"), up from #5 six months ago.

5. [[ai-agents-in-gtm]] shows that AI agent pricing is still unsettled across the market. No consensus model exists. Buyers know prices will drop, which makes them reluctant to sign long-term contracts at current rates.

## Supporting Data

- From [[hubspot]]: 20-40% effective price increase for mid-market teams with 30+ seats under the new per-seat model. Source: [[2026-04-05-customer-churn-analysis-q1]]
- From [[pricing-model-shifts]]: Snowflake data shows 78% of enterprise customers prefer consumption pricing. This preference is filtering down to mid-market tool decisions. Source: [[2026-04-02-snowflake-consumption-model-analysis]]
- From [[salesforce]]: $2/conversation pricing for Agentforce sets a reference point. Buyers now ask other vendors: "Why don't you charge based on results?" Source: [[2026-04-08-salesforce-agentforce-pricing]]
- From [[hubspot]]: Churn in Q1 2026 was 15% higher than Q1 2025 among mid-market accounts (100-500 employees). Pricing cited as primary reason in 62% of exit surveys. Source: [[2026-04-05-customer-churn-analysis-q1]]

## Implications

- **Competitive positioning:** For the next 6-12 months, leading with pricing model as a differentiator is unusually effective. Buyers are primed to hear alternatives.
- **Outbound timing:** Target companies that renewed HubSpot or Salesforce contracts in Q3-Q4 2025 (before the full impact of pricing changes hit). They're now seeing the cost impact and haven't locked into new contracts yet.
- **Content angle:** "The Great GTM Repricing" is a content thesis that can sustain 8-10 pieces. Every new vendor pricing change adds fuel. This aligns with the [[trust-architecture]] principle: go deep on one angle rather than covering many lightly.
- **Product strategy:** If you're launching a GTM product in 2026, usage-based or outcome-based pricing isn't just a preference. It's a competitive requirement. Per-seat will be a sales objection.

## Open Questions

- How long does the switching window stay open? Once buyers settle into new contracts (likely Q4 2026), the window closes until the next round of pricing disruption.
- Will Salesforce's $2/conversation model actually scale? If early Agentforce adopters see poor ROI, outcome-based pricing could stall rather than spread.
- Are mid-market buyers actually switching, or just evaluating? Churn data from HubSpot suggests real movement, but we need data from 2-3 more vendors to confirm this is market-wide.
