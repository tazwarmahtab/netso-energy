# Pricing Model Shifts

## Pattern Name and Description

B2B SaaS vendors are moving away from flat per-seat pricing toward usage-based, outcome-based, or hybrid models. This isn't just a pricing trend. It changes how buyers evaluate tools, how sales teams structure deals, and how competitors position against each other.

The shift is happening at two levels. Infrastructure and data companies (Snowflake, AWS, OpenAI) moved to consumption pricing years ago. What's new is GTM tools following the same path. When Salesforce prices Agentforce at $2/conversation instead of per-seat, and HubSpot restructures from contact-based to per-seat, the entire mid-market GTM stack reprices within 12-18 months because buyers start expecting the same flexibility from every vendor.

## Evidence

- 2025-09-15: HubSpot eliminated legacy contact-based tiers and moved all hubs to per-seat pricing. Framed as "simplification" but effectively raised costs for teams with many seats and few contacts. Source: [[2026-03-22-hubspot-pricing-page-capture]]
- 2026-01-15: Salesforce launched Agentforce with outcome-based pricing at $2 per conversation. First major CRM to decouple pricing from headcount entirely for an AI product. Source: [[2026-04-08-salesforce-agentforce-pricing]]
- 2026-02-01: OpenAI continued aggressive API price cuts (GPT-4 Turbo dropped 60% in 12 months) while shifting enterprise contracts to token-based consumption. This trains buyers to expect usage-based pricing from any AI-powered tool. Source: [[2026-03-05-openai-api-pricing-usage-shift]]
- 2026-03-10: Snowflake reported that 78% of enterprise customers preferred consumption pricing over annual commits in their Q4 2025 survey. This data point circulates in board decks and influences GTM tool procurement decisions. Source: [[2026-04-02-snowflake-consumption-model-analysis]]

## Frequency

4 of 7 tracked GTM vendors have made significant pricing model changes in the last 6 months. Of those 4, 3 moved toward some form of usage or outcome-based component. The remaining 3 vendors (Outreach, Gong, Apollo) still use pure per-seat pricing but all three have hinted at changes in earnings calls or product announcements.

## Trend

- **Direction:** Rising
- **First observed:** 2025-09-15 (HubSpot pricing restructure was the trigger that made this a pattern, not a one-off)
- **Time period tracked:** 7 months (September 2025 to April 2026)
- **Notes:** The pattern is accelerating. The gap between HubSpot's move (September 2025) and Salesforce's move (January 2026) was 4 months. Expect remaining holdouts to adjust by Q3 2026.

## Implications

- **For positioning:** If you sell a GTM tool with per-seat pricing, you need a clear justification for why per-seat makes sense for the buyer. "Because that's how SaaS works" stopped being convincing when Salesforce broke the model.
- **For outbound:** Pricing model is now a competitive differentiator, not just a procurement detail. If your prospect is on HubSpot and frustrated by the per-seat increase, leading with "we charge based on what you actually use" is a strong opening angle.
- **For content:** Pricing-related content performs well right now. Our posts about HubSpot's pricing change and Salesforce's $2/conversation model got 3-4x typical engagement. Buyers are actively researching this. Source: [[2026-03-15-hubspot-annual-report-2025]]
- **For product:** If you're building pricing models for a new GTM product, default to usage-based or hybrid. Pure per-seat is already starting to feel dated in this market.
