# Business Model Architecture & Business Plan for Netso Solar Pergolas (Bangladesh)

## 1. Executive Summary

Netso Solar Pergolas aims to become Bangladesh’s leading premium rooftop solar infrastructure brand. Our core product combines electricity generation with architectural shade structures, delivering a multipurpose solution that adds value and aesthetics to buildings. With Bangladesh’s rooftop solar capacity still in its infancy—only **245 MW** installed as of **June 2025**【849026557008993†L92-L105】—and the government launching a national rooftop programme that mandates solar panels on public buildings【274731815190776†L196-L209】, opportunities for well‑executed projects are expanding. Netso will capitalise on policy tailwinds, rising electricity tariffs, and increasing climate awareness by offering modular, engineered pergolas backed by financing and long‑term service.

## 3. Market Opportunity & Context

Bangladesh’s renewable energy policy environment is shifting rapidly. The **Renewable Energy Policy 2025** aims for **20 %** of electricity generation from renewables by **2030** and **30 %** by **2040**【274731815190776†L196-L198】. As of June 2025, Bangladesh had only **245 MW** of rooftop solar capacity【849026557008993†L92-L105】, compared with neighbouring countries where solar accounts for **17–39 %** of electricity【274731815190776†L192-L194】.  

In June 2025 the government launched the **National Rooftop Solar Programme**, directing authorities to install solar panels on **all government buildings** and public institutions【274731815190776†L196-L209】. The programme encourages private companies to install and maintain systems on government roofs, with institutions receiving **rent** for rooftop use【274731815190776†L211-L223】.  

Net‑metering guidelines were initially set in 2018 with a cap at **70 %** of sanctioned load; revisions underway in 2025 are expected to allow installations up to **100 %** of the sanctioned load【849026557008993†L129-L147】, boosting project economics. These policy tailwinds, combined with rising electricity tariffs and greater awareness of climate resilience, create a substantial market for well‑executed rooftop solar solutions.

## 4. Target Customer Segments

Netso will prioritise segments where solar pergolas deliver both functional and aesthetic value and where customers have the capacity to pay or access financing:

- Upper‑middle‑class homeowners seeking shade, comfort and long‑term electricity savings.
- Hospitality venues such as restaurants, rooftop cafés, boutique hotels and resorts that want attractive outdoor spaces while reducing energy costs.
- Small and medium‑sized enterprises (SMEs) and corporate offices looking to improve energy independence and corporate sustainability.
- Public institutions—schools, colleges, hospitals—participating in government rooftop programmes with private OPEX/lease arrangements.

## 5. Value Proposition & Product Strategy

Netso’s value proposition rests on delivering solar as a premium lifestyle product rather than a commodity. Key elements include:

- **Solar Pergola SKUs:** A limited range of pre‑engineered pergolas with different span sizes, roof pitches and finishes, designed to withstand local wind loads and monsoon conditions.
- **Aesthetic & Comfort:** Pergolas provide shade, usable outdoor space and architectural enhancement while housing high‑efficiency PV modules.
- **Turnkey Service:** End‑to‑end execution—site survey, 3D design visualisation, permitting, subsidy paperwork, installation, net‑metering application and commissioning.
- **Financing Options:** Upfront purchase (CAPEX) with extended warranty or lease/OPEX models where monthly payments are lower than electricity savings.
- **Performance Guarantee & Monitoring:** Remote monitoring system with energy dashboards and service alerts; maintenance contracts ensure uptime.
- **Quality Assurance:** Rigid installation checklist and photographic documentation at each stage to build trust.

## 6. Go‑to‑Market Strategy

The sales and marketing strategy emphasises building trust and showcasing the lifestyle benefits of pergolas:

- **Digital Marketing & Content:** Develop a website and social media presence with 3D renders, testimonials and generation data. Use targeted online advertising to reach affluent neighbourhoods and commercial clients.
- **Partnerships:** Collaborate with architects, interior designers and real‑estate developers to integrate pergolas into new construction and renovation projects.
- **Channel Sales:** Establish a B2B sales team to approach hotels, restaurants and corporate offices. Participate in government tenders for rooftop programmes.
- **Demonstration Sites:** Install pilot pergolas at high‑visibility locations (e.g., cafés, community parks) to showcase aesthetics and performance.
- **Referral & Incentive Programmes:** Encourage satisfied customers to refer peers by offering service discounts or small referral fees.
- **Events & Public Relations:** Host workshops on net‑metering benefits and renewable energy policy, leveraging media coverage of Bangladesh’s rooftop solar push.

## 7. Operational Plan & Phases

Netso will scale operations in phases to prove the model, build capabilities and capture market share efficiently.

### Phase 1: Validation (0–2 months)

- Finalise pergola SKUs and structural designs, incorporating wind‑load calculations and material specifications.
- Develop a quality assurance (QA) checklist and photo‑based handover process.
- Secure supply agreements with module, inverter and structural fabricators.
- Complete three pilot installations: one premium residence, one hospitality venue and one SME/commercial roof to gather data and testimonials.
- Establish relationships with banks or microfinance institutions to structure lease/EMI products.

### Phase 2: Commercialisation (2–6 months)

- Launch marketing campaigns and demonstration sites to build brand awareness.
- Sign distribution/installation agreements with architects, real estate developers and hospitality chains.
- Offer CAPEX and OPEX financing options with clear ROI calculations demonstrating savings vs. payments.
- Implement remote monitoring platform and maintenance service structure.
- Train installation teams and enforce QA processes to maintain quality at scale.

### Phase 3: Institutional & Scale‑Up (6–12 months)

- Bid for government and institutional rooftop projects under the National Rooftop Solar Programme.
- Expand manufacturing capacity by exploring local fabrication to reduce import duties and costs.
- Develop energy storage and EV charging add‑ons to increase revenue per installation.
- Establish regional operations teams to expand beyond the initial city while maintaining installation density.
- Prepare for Series A funding to finance inventory, working capital and technology development.

## 8. Supply Chain & Partnerships

A robust supply chain is critical for cost control and quality assurance. Netso will:

- Negotiate bulk procurement agreements with Tier‑1 solar module and inverter manufacturers to ensure high efficiency and warranty support.
- Partner with local steel and aluminium fabricators to produce pergola structures, reducing exposure to import duties and currency fluctuations.
- Collaborate with banks and green financing programmes to provide lease/EMI products and reduce the barrier of upfront costs.
- Work closely with government agencies to streamline net‑metering applications and approvals.
- Establish a network of trained installation contractors bound by strict QA and safety standards.

## 10. Risk Assessment & Mitigation

- **Engineering & Structural Risk:** Inadequate design could lead to structural failure during storms.  
  *Mitigation:* Engage structural engineers to certify designs; perform wind‑load analysis and adhere to building codes.
- **Quality Control & Reputation:** Poor installations or equipment failures may damage brand trust.  
  *Mitigation:* Implement strict QA processes with photo documentation; train and certify installers; use Tier‑1 components.
- **Regulatory & Policy Changes:** Delays or changes in net‑metering guidelines could affect project economics.  
  *Mitigation:* Maintain relationships with regulators; diversify revenue streams across CAPEX/OPEX; stay agile in adjusting capacity to fit caps.
- **Financing & Payment Risk:** Customers may struggle to make lease payments if electricity prices fall or economic conditions deteriorate.  
  *Mitigation:* Use conservative savings assumptions; obtain credit checks; partner with banks for risk sharing; offer CAPEX option.
- **Supply Chain Disruption:** Import duties or global shortages may increase costs【849026557008993†L129-L146】.  
  *Mitigation:* Source locally manufactured structures; maintain inventory buffers; diversify suppliers.
- **Execution Risk:** Rapid scale could strain operations and lead to delays.  
  *Mitigation:* Scale gradually; prioritise quality over quantity; invest in project management software.

## 11. Sustainability & ESG Impact

Netso contributes to Bangladesh’s renewable energy targets by accelerating rooftop solar adoption. Delivering over **50 installations** in the first year could add ~3 MW of solar capacity, helping reduce dependence on fossil fuels and aligning with national goals of **20 %** renewable electricity by **2030**【274731815190776†L196-L198】. Using recyclable aluminium and steel, implementing safe e‑waste practices for modules and inverters, and empowering local technicians through training will further enhance ESG credentials.
