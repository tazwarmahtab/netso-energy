## PART 2 (EXPANDED): PLATFORM FEATURES & CAPABILITIES

### 2.5 Net Metering Policy Foundation

**Netso's entire business model is enabled by Bangladesh's Net Metering policy.**

#### What is Net Metering?

> **Net Metering**: A solar system generates electricity. The building consumes what it needs. Excess electricity flows to the national grid. The utility provides credits or payment for that exported electricity.

**How It Works**:
1. **Generation**: Solar panels produce DC electricity during daylight hours
2. **Conversion**: Inverter converts DC to AC (synchronized with grid frequency)
3. **Consumption**: Building consumes electricity first (offsetting grid purchases)
4. **Export**: Excess electricity flows to national grid (reverse meter flow)
5. **Credits**: Utility tracks exported kWh; provides 1:1 credit against future consumption
6. **Settlement**: At billing cycle end, customer pays only for "net" consumption

**Example**:
- **Daytime**: 30 kWh generated, 10 kWh consumed on-site → 20 kWh exported
- **Nighttime**: 0 kWh generated, 15 kWh consumed from grid
- **Net**: 20 kWh exported - 15 kWh imported = 5 kWh credit (carried forward)
- **Billing**: Customer receives credit for 5 kWh at end of month

#### Regulatory Framework

| Authority | Role | Responsibility |
|-----------|------|----------------|
| **Bangladesh Power Development Board (BPDB)** | Primary Regulator | Net metering approval, PPA execution, billing & settlement |
| **Sustainable and Renewable Energy Development Authority (SREDA)** | Secondary Authority | Equipment certification, installer accreditation, policy development |
| **Power Division** | Ministry Oversight | Policy direction, application portal management |

#### Application Process

**Portal**: [nem.powerdivision.gov.bd](https://nem.powerdivision.gov.bd) (Net Metering Application System)

**Steps**:
1. **Pre-Application**: Site assessment, system design, equipment selection
2. **Online Application**: Submit via nem.powerdivision.gov.bd
3. **Technical Review**: BPDB engineering team reviews system design
4. **Approval**: Interconnection agreement issued (typically 30-60 days)
5. **Installation**: Certified installer completes installation
6. **Inspection**: BPDB/SREDA inspector verifies compliance
7. **Meter Exchange**: Utility replaces standard meter with bi-directional net meter
8. **Commissioning**: System energized, net metering begins

**Timeline**: 60-90 days from application to commissioning (typical)

**Key Requirement**: All equipment MUST be SREDA-approved. BPDB will reject applications with non-certified panels or inverters.

## PART 3: BUSINESS MODEL & REVENUE ARCHITECTURE

### 3.1 Three Business Models — Ranked by Strategic Value

| Model | Name | How It Works | Revenue Type | Strategic Value | Valuation Multiple |
|-------|------|--------------|--------------|-----------------|-------------------|
| **Model A** | **Installer** | Customer pays upfront. Netso installs. One-time revenue. | One-time margin (15-25%) | **Weak** — commodity, no recurring value, capped by labor | 1-2x revenue |
| **Model B** | **Solar Leasing** | Netso owns system. Customer pays monthly lease (lower than grid bill). | Recurring lease payments (10-20 yr) | **Strong** — recurring revenue, asset building, customer lock-in | 5-8x revenue |
| **Model C** | **Asset Ownership** | Netso finances, owns, and operates solar assets. Revenue from electricity sales (PPA). | 25-year PPA cashflow | **Best** — infrastructure company valuation, scalable with capital | 10-15x revenue |

#### Model A: Installer (Weak)
- **Pros**: Immediate cash flow, low capital requirement, simple execution
- **Cons**: Commodity business, price competition, no recurring revenue, valuation ceiling
- **When to Use**: Early stage (Year 0-2) to prove capability, generate cash flow, build portfolio
- **Risk**: Race to bottom on price; dependent on continuous new customer acquisition

#### Model B: Solar Leasing (Strong)
- **Pros**: Recurring revenue, customer retention (10-20 yr leases), asset accumulation, higher valuation
- **Cons**: Requires capital (system cost upfront), credit risk (customer default), balance sheet intensity
- **When to Use**: Growth stage (Year 2-5) with access to debt financing (IDCOL, BIFFL, banks)
- **Risk**: Interest rate risk, customer credit risk, early lease termination

#### Model C: Asset Ownership (Best)
- **Pros**: 25-year predictable cashflow, infrastructure valuation (10-15x revenue), scalable with institutional capital, VPP optionality
- **Cons**: Significant capital requirement ($500K-$1M/MW), long payback (7-10 years), balance sheet intensity
- **When to Use**: Scale stage (Year 5+) with institutional capital access (climate funds, infrastructure PE)
- **Risk**: Policy risk (net metering changes), technology risk (panel degradation), interest rate risk

**Netso Strategy**: 
> **Start with Model A** (installer) to prove capability and generate cash flow → **Transition to Model B** (leasing) as debt financing becomes available → **Scale with Model C** (asset ownership) once institutional capital is accessible.

**Recommended Path**: Model C (Asset Ownership) + Model B (Leasing) in early stages, shifting fully to infrastructure ownership as capital scales.

### 4.3 SAM — Serviceable Addressable Market

**Definition**: The portion of TAM that is **structurally viable and reachable** — accounting for structural limitations, regulatory approvals, owner willingness, and financing access.

| Metric | Value | % of TAM | Notes |
|--------|-------|----------|-------|
| **SAM Capacity** | 1,200–1,600 MW | 30-40% | Structurally viable rooftops |
| **SAM Revenue (Hardware)** | $1.2–1.6 Billion | 30-40% | Installable with current technology |
| **SAM Revenue (BDT)** | 130–180 Billion BDT | 30-40% | At current exchange rates |
| **Annual Energy Value** | 1,680–2,240 GWh/year | 30-40% | 1,400 kWh/kW × 1,200-1,600 MW |

**SAM Filters Applied**:

#### Filter 1: Structural Viability (60% of TAM)
- **Roof Condition**: Must be structurally sound (no major repairs needed)
- **Shading**: <20% shading from adjacent buildings/trees
- **Orientation**: South-facing optimal (East/West acceptable with yield penalty)
- **Access**: Safe rooftop access for installation + maintenance
- **Result**: Eliminates ~40% of TAM (poor roof conditions, heavy shading)

#### Filter 2: Grid Capacity (80% of remaining)
- **Grid Absorption**: Local substation must handle bidirectional flow
- **Transformer Capacity**: Distribution transformer must not overload
- **Net Metering Cap**: Current policy caps at ~3% of peak demand (relaxing)
- **Result**: Eliminates ~20% of TAM (grid-constrained areas)

#### Filter 3: Financing Access (70% of remaining)
- **Consumer Credit**: Homeowners must access loans (or Netso provides PPA)
- **Upfront Capital**: ৳300K-600K for 3-5 kWp residential (prohibitive for most)
- **Commercial Financing**: Factories need 7-10 year tenors at 9-11% interest
- **Result**: Eliminates ~20-30% of TAM (credit-constrained owners)

#### Filter 4: Owner Willingness (85% of remaining)
- **Awareness**: Must understand net metering economics
- **Trust**: Must trust installer quality (25-year asset)
- **Motivation**: Must value long-term savings over short-term cost
- **Result**: Eliminates ~10-15% of TAM (unwilling/unsuitable owners)
