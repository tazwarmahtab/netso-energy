## PART 6: MARKET SIZING & SEGMENTATION

### 6.1 TAM — Total Addressable Market

**Definition**: The total theoretical demand for rooftop solar in Bangladesh if every viable rooftop were equipped with solar panels.

| Metric | Value | Notes |
|--------|-------|-------|
| **TAM Capacity** | 40-50 GW | Technical potential (all urban rooftops) |
| **TAM Revenue** | $32-40B | At ৳80-100K/kWp ($0.8-1M/MW) |
| **Rooftop Area** | ~4,000 km² | Urban Bangladesh (Dhaka, Chattogram, Gazipur, etc.) |
| **Assumption** | 10-12 W/m² | Typical rooftop solar density |

**Key Insight**: 
> TAM represents the ceiling, not the target. It answers "how big could this get if everything went perfectly?"

**Why TAM Matters**:
- Shows investors the ultimate scale potential
- Validates Bangladesh as a meaningful solar market
- Attracts infrastructure-scale capital (pension funds, sovereign wealth)

**Caveats**:
- Assumes 100% rooftop adoption (unrealistic)
- Ignores grid constraints (BPDB absorption capacity)
- Ignores financing constraints (consumer access to capital)
- Ignores regulatory barriers (net metering caps, approval bottlenecks)

### 6.2 SAM — Serviceable Addressable Market

**Definition**: The portion of TAM that is **structurally viable and reachable** — accounting for structural limitations, regulatory approvals, owner willingness, and financing access.

| Metric | Value | Notes |
|--------|-------|-------|
| **SAM Capacity** | 1,200-1,600 MW | 30-40% of TAM |
| **SAM Revenue** | $1.2-1.6B | At current pricing |
| **Penetration Rate** | 30-40% | Realistic adoption ceiling (near-term) |
| **Key Filters Applied** | Structural viability, BPDB approval capacity, financing access |

**Key Filters Applied to Derive SAM**:

#### Filter 1: Structural Viability
- **Roof Condition**: Must be structurally sound (no major repairs needed)
- **Shading**: <20% shading from adjacent buildings/trees
- **Orientation**: South-facing optimal (East/West acceptable with yield penalty)
- **Access**: Safe rooftop access for installation + maintenance
- **Result**: Eliminates ~40% of TAM (poor roof conditions, heavy shading)

#### Filter 2: BPDB Approval Capacity
- **Grid Absorption**: Local substation must handle bidirectional flow
- **Transformer Capacity**: Distribution transformer must not overload
- **Net Metering Cap**: Current policy caps at ~3% of peak demand (relaxing)
- **Approval Backlog**: SREDA/BPDB processing capacity (~500 systems/year currently)
- **Result**: Eliminates ~20% of TAM (grid-constrained areas)

#### Filter 3: Financing Access
- **Consumer Credit**: Homeowners must access loans (or Netso provides PPA)
- **Upfront Capital**: ৳400K-600K for 5kWp residential (prohibitive for most)
- **Commercial Financing**: Factories need 7-10 year tenors at 9-11% interest
- **Result**: Eliminates ~20-30% of TAM (credit-constrained owners)

#### Filter 4: Owner Willingness
- **Awareness**: Must understand net metering economics
- **Trust**: Must trust installer quality (25-year asset)
- **Motivation**: Must value long-term savings over short-term cost
- **Result**: Eliminates ~10-15% of TAM (unwilling/unsuitable owners)

**SAM Calculation**:
```
TAM: 40-50 GW
× Structural Viability (60%): 24-30 GW
× Grid Capacity (80%): 19-24 GW
× Financing Access (70%): 13-17 GW
× Owner Willingness (85%): 11-14 GW
SAM: 1.2-1.6 GW (conservative, near-term)
```

**Strategic Insight**:
> SAM is the **realistic near-term market** (5-10 years). Netso capturing 1-2% of SAM = 12-32 MW portfolio.

### 6.5 Market Growth Drivers

#### Driver 1: Rising Electricity Demand
- **Trend**: 7-9% annual growth in electricity demand
- **Cause**: Urbanization, AC adoption, industrialization, EV future
- **Impact**: Grid strain, load shedding, tariff increases
- **Netso Opportunity**: Distributed generation at point of use reduces grid stress

#### Driver 2: Solar Panel Cost Decline
- **Trend**: 85% cost reduction over past decade (2010-2025)
- **Current Price**: $0.15-0.20/W (utility-scale), $0.25-0.35/W (rooftop)
- **Impact**: Grid parity achieved in most markets
- **Netso Opportunity**: Competitive vs. grid tariffs (৳8-12/kWh)

#### Driver 3: Government Policy Push
- **Target**: 3,000 MW rooftop solar by 2030 (SREDA)
- **Mechanism**: Net metering, tax incentives, green financing
- **Status**: Framework established, implementation scaling
- **Netso Opportunity**: First-mover advantage in compliant installations

#### Driver 4: International ESG Pressure
- **Trend**: Global buyers (H&M, Nike, Walmart) demanding supplier decarbonization
- **Mechanism**: RE100 commitments, Scope 3 emissions reporting
- **Impact**: Garment factories need renewable energy credentials
- **Netso Opportunity**: Factory rooftop solar = ESG compliance + cost savings

#### Driver 5: Net Metering Framework
- **Status**: Established by BPDB (2019, updated 2023)
- **Mechanism**: 1:1 credit for exported kWh, 20-year PPA
- **Capacity**: ~3% of peak demand (relaxing)
- **Netso Opportunity**: Regulatory certainty enables financing

#### Driver 6: Energy Import Dependency
- **Trend**: Bangladesh imports 60%+ of primary energy (LNG, coal, oil)
- **Vulnerability**: Forex pressure, geopolitical risk, price volatility
- **Policy**: Reduce import dependency via domestic renewables
- **Netso Opportunity**: Energy security narrative (national interest)

### 6.6 Market Barriers

| Barrier | Description | Impact | Mitigation Strategy |
|---------|-------------|--------|---------------------|
| **Upfront Capital** | Solar requires ৳400K-600K for 5kWp residential; most owners cannot pay upfront | Eliminates 70-80% of potential customers | PPA/leasing model (IDCOL/BIFFL financing), bank partnerships, milestone payments |
| **Bureaucratic Approvals** | SREDA + BPDB permitting causes 3-6 month delays; inconsistent enforcement | Slows deployment, increases working capital needs | Dedicated compliance team, pre-approved designs, relationship building with regulators |
| **Consumer Education** | Many building owners don't understand net metering ROI; fear of complexity | Long sales cycles, high customer acquisition cost | Educational content, case studies, site visits to existing installs, ROI calculators |
| **Policy Risk** | Rule changes could affect economics (e.g., net metering caps, tariff reductions) | Investor uncertainty, financing challenges | Diversify into off-grid + storage, lobby through industry associations, hedge via PPAs |
| **Engineering Trust** | New brand must prove quality to overcome hesitancy (25-year asset) | Price pressure, preference for established installers | Tier-1 suppliers (Jinko, Sungrow), 25yr performance warranty, third-party insurance, visible portfolio |
| **Grid Constraints** | Local transformers/substations may not handle bidirectional flow | Installation delays, curtailment risk | Early grid impact studies, engage BPDB in planning, target grid-strong areas first |
| **Roof Condition** | Many rooftops need repairs before solar installation | Adds cost/complexity, delays projects | Partner with roofing contractors, offer bundled roof repair + solar, structural assessment upfront |

## PART 7: STRATEGIC ARCHITECTURE — 0 TO 100 MW ROADMAP

### 7.1 The Aggregation Thesis

**Core Insight**: Single rooftops are small. **The power of Netso is aggregation.**

This is the fundamental insight that separates an **installer** from an **infrastructure company**.

| Scale | Capacity | Analogy |
|-------|----------|---------|
| 1 rooftop | 3 kW | One drop |
| 100 rooftops | 300 kW | Small stream |
| 1,000 rooftops | 3 MW | River |
| 10,000 rooftops | 30 MW | Major waterway |
| 33,000+ rooftops | 100 MW | **Virtual Power Plant** |

**Why Aggregation Matters**:
1. **Economies of Scale**: Bulk purchasing, standardized processes, lower O&M costs
2. **Financing Access**: Institutional capital requires $5M+ portfolios
3. **Grid Services**: 10+ MW can provide ancillary services (frequency regulation, peak shaving)
4. **Data Network**: 1,000+ rooftops = predictive analytics, performance optimization
5. **Market Power**: 100 MW = negotiate directly with suppliers, utilities, policymakers

**Strategic Imperative**: 
> Do not think "one install at a time." Think "portfolio of 10,000 rooftops." Every process, contract, and technology decision must scale to 100 MW.

---

### 7.2 Cluster Expansion Strategy

**Principle**: Solar adoption spreads **geographically through social proof**. The correct strategy is **hyper-local cluster dominance** — not scattered city-wide installs.

**Why Clusters?**
- **Visibility**: 10 installs in one neighborhood = 10x word-of-mouth
- **Efficiency**: One crew, one location, lower travel/setup time
- **Trust**: "My neighbor did it" > "Someone in another district did it"
- **Grid**: Easier to manage grid impact in concentrated area
- **Marketing**: Door-to-door, community events, local influencers
