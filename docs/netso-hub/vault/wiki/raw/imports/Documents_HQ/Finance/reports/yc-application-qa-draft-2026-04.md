## Section 2: What does your company do? (50 words)

Netso installs solar panel canopies on the rooftops of Bangladesh garment factories, operates them under 25-year government-backed PPA contracts, and sells the clean energy to grid and factory at BERC-regulated tariffs. We make money on installation margin + 25-year recurring revenue streams. No capex required from the customer. Research-validated unit economics: 19% IRR, 5.4yr payback.

---

## Section 3: Tell us about the problem (the personal version)

Bangladesh generates electricity at $0.09–0.15/kWh but garment factories pay $0.18–0.25/kWh after transmission losses and demand charges — among the highest electricity costs per unit of output in the world. These factories control their own rooftops, which sit empty in 3,200 hours of annual sunlight.

My family has operated garment factories in the Chattogram RMG cluster for 20 years. I've seen those rooftots sit empty my entire life. A solar canopy converts wasted roof space into a 25-year income stream for the landlord and cuts the factory's electricity cost by 15–20%. Both of them win.

No one is systematizing this in Bangladesh yet — <2.5% rooftop penetration, 370 MW installed vs. 4,000 MW potential.

## Section 4: The most important thing to know about Netso (YC personal note — from Tazwar)

I have privileged, zero-cold-outreach access to 20–30 garment factory rooftops in the Chattogram RMG cluster through my family's industrial network. This is not a competitive moat — it's a starting advantage that compresses our customer acquisition cost from $2,000+ per deal to ~$130 per deal on the primary channel, because the first cohort comes from people who already know my family.

The actual moat is regulatory: under Bangladesh's BERC net metering framework, a rooftop solar PPA is a 25-year contract backed by government-fixed electricity tariffs. This means every deal we sign today has predictable, inflation-linked revenue for a quarter century.

Our 3kW pilot system has been running since December 2025. The numbers check out.

## Section 10: How will you make money?

Three revenue streams per rooftop:

1. **EPC installation margin** — profit on solar equipment + installation (~15% of CAPEX, one-time)

2. **PPA generation revenue** — 25 years of recurring income from every commissioned rooftop:
   - 80% of generation sold to grid at BERC net metering rate (৳9.32/kWh)
   - 20% offsets factory's retail electricity cost (৳12.50/kWh)
   - Year 1 gross revenue per 100kW rooftop: ৳15.4 lakhs
   - Revenue escalates with BERC tariff adjustments (~2%/yr)

3. **Asset portfolio appreciation** — each commissioned 100kW rooftop adds ~$39K (৳46.8L) to Netso's balance sheet. At 1 MW (10 rooftops), portfolio asset value ~$390K. At 30 MW: infrastructure-scale valuation ($5M+).

*Path to revenue*: Signed PPA → IDCOL equipment financing (~8 weeks) → Installation → SREDA commissioning → Generation revenue begins. First paying customer expected Q2–Q3 2026.

## Section 14: Anything else we should know?

Netso is not competing with the global solar market. We are capturing a specific, addressable slice: Bangladesh's garment manufacturing corridor, which is the second largest in the world and operates under intense global scrutiny on energy and emissions. Brands like H&M, Zara, and Nike have committed to RE100 — but their Bangladesh suppliers have no path to cost-effective solar without operators like us.

We are not selling panels. We are building infrastructure. Each rooftop is a 25-year contracted cash flow with a government-backed de facto floor on electricity prices. That is the product.
