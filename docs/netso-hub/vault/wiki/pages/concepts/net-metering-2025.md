# Net Metering Guidelines 2025 (Bangladesh)

SREDA's net metering framework underwent a major overhaul in 2025/2026, transitioning from a restrictive industrial model to a broad multi-sector model that enables Netso's business.

## Thesis

Net Metering 2.0 removes the structural restrictions that previously limited rooftop solar adoption in Bangladesh — universal eligibility, 100% load matching, direct cash payout for surplus — making the RESCO model viable at residential scale.

## Key 2025/2026 Updates

### System Capacity Limits (CONFIRMED)
- **Maximum capacity:** 1 MW (1000 kW) per consumer
- **Minimum capacity:** 1 kW
- **Eligible consumers:** All consumer categories (residential, commercial, industrial) across all voltage levels
- **Source:** Official NEM Portal FAQ (nem.powerdivision.gov.bd)

### Eligibility Expansion
- **Old:** Primarily limited to three-phase industrial and commercial consumers
- **New:** Open to **single-phase and three-phase** consumers across residential, commercial, and industrial segments

This is the single most important expansion for Netso's go-to-market. It unlocks the residential segment that was previously excluded.

### 100% Load Matching
- **Old:** Capped at 70% of sanctioned load
- **New:** Systems can be designed for **up to 100% of the consumer's sanctioned load**

Enables larger system designs. Higher kW per installation → higher revenue per customer → better unit economics.

### Prepaid and Smart Meter Compatibility
Full technical compatibility with modern smart-meter and prepaid-meter systems. Removes a historical billing-infrastructure bottleneck that blocked many solar conversions.

### Direct Cash Settlement for Surplus
- **Old:** Surplus electricity only generated bill credit
- **New:** Surplus can be **paid directly to the consumer's bank account or mobile wallet (MFS: bKash, Nagod)**

Improves cash flow for Netso on the surplus sell-back. No waiting for bill cycle.

### Mandatory Rooftop Solar
Solar installation is now mandatory for new electricity connections for buildings with **at least 1,000 sq. ft. of usable roof space**. This creates a mandated inflow of potential customers — new construction that is legally required to consider solar.

### Investment Models (CONFIRMED)
The guidelines explicitly support two models:

**1. CAPEX Model:**
- Customer owns the system
- Customer bears upfront cost
- Customer gets all benefits

**2. OPEX Model (RESCO/Third-Party Ownership):**
- Third-party (Netso) owns the system
- Customer pays only for electricity consumed (PPA rate)
- No upfront investment by customer
- This is Netso's primary model

## Regulatory Bodies

- **SREDA** (Sustainable and Renewable Energy Development Authority) — Policy setter
- **BERC** (Bangladesh Energy Regulatory Commission) — Tariff approval, dispute resolution
- **BPDB** (Bangladesh Power Development Board) — Grid operator, NTDCL manages interconnection
- **DESCO / DPDC / NESCO** — Distribution companies handling connection agreements by zone

## Connection Process (RESCO Requirements)

Netso must:
1. Obtain grid interconnection approval from the relevant DESCO/DPDC/NESCO
2. Register the system with SREDA under net metering
3. Sign a 20-25 year PPA with the customer
4. Meter setup: bidirectional meter that tracks both import and export

Approval timeline: **3-6 months** (known bottleneck — flag for pipeline planning).

## Evidence For

- Framework change is SREDA/official — high confidence, authoritative source
- Single-phase eligibility expansion directly enables Netso's residential ICP
- Mandatory rooftop solar mandate creates forced-funnel demand

## Evidence Against

- Timeline for local DESCO/DPDC/NESCO implementation may lag SREDA policy — practical rollout speed uncertain
- BERC tariff approval process could constrain the pricing Netso can offer customers

## How Netso Uses It

- For **trust-first sales conversations**: customers unfamiliar with solar law don't know these rights exist — educating them on the legal framework builds credibility
- In **objection handling**: when customers worry about grid reliability, Net Metering 2.0 framework provides government backing
- For **ICP identification**: mandatory rooftop rule means any new construction >=1,000 sq ft is a potential lead
- In **deal structuring**: 100% load matching enables larger system proposals → better IRR per installation
