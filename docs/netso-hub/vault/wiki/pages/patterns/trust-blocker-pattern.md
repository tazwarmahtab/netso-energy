# Trust Blocker Pattern

**High-bill residential and commercial customers in Bangladesh hesitate on solar not because of price, but because of distrust of solar vendors.**

## Pattern Description

Past experience with fly-by-night solar installers — companies that oversell, underdeliver, use substandard equipment, and disappear after installation — has created deep skepticism across Bangladesh's electricity consumers. The trust gap is the primary conversion blocker, not unit economics.

**What this looks like in conversation:**
- Customer asks "will you still be here in 10 years?"
- Customer cites a friend's bad solar experience
- Customer is skeptical of "free" or "zero upfront cost" offers
- Customer wants to see an installed system before committing
- Customer asks about guarantees, warranty, and maintenance

## Evidence

As of April 2026, the primary conversion challenge for rooftop solar in Bangladesh is credibility — not pricing or savings. This is documented across Netso intelligence from the Atlas wiki and VC/finance reviews.

**Supporting pattern:** High-bill (৳40-80k) households have the most to gain financially but are also the most risk-averse — they have more to lose from a failed installation.

## Frequency

High — observed across all prospect segments. The pattern is consistent across: residential homeowners, commercial building owners, and industrial operators.

## Implications for Netso's GTM

1. **Sales pitch must lead with trust, not savings.** Leading with ROI ("save ৳X per month") triggers the skepticism reflex. Leading with credibility (pilot system, O&M contract, 25-year commitment) opens the conversation.

2. **Social proof is critical.** A working 4kW pilot system in the same area as a prospect is the strongest trust signal. Testimonials, before/after bills, and references from existing installations convert far better than financial projections.

3. **Zero upfront model is double-edged.** It removes the capital barrier but triggers "too good to be true" suspicion. The pitch must explicitly address why Netso can offer this — system ownership + long-term revenue from electricity sales.

4. **O&M commitment is a trust signal.** The ৳5,000/system/year O&M contract (included in standard PPA) is not just a revenue item — it's proof Netso will still be around to service the system.

5. **Physical presence and local references beat digital presentations.** Prospects want to see a real installer, in a real building, answering real questions.
